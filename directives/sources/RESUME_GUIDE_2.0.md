## 2. How the Corporate World Hires
When a company has an open position, they give a manager the budget for a new hire. This manager is called a **Hiring Manager (HM)**.
* The HM is just a regular manager who needs to hire. It is rarely their entire job, and most HMs do not have significant hiring experience (some may only hire once every few years).
* To help with this process, HMs engage **Recruiters** to manage the company's open positions.

### The Corporate Hiring Steps
1. HM gets budget for a new role.
2. HM engages a Recruiter.
3. Recruiter gathers the qualifications (keywords) for the role from the HM.
4. Recruiter posts a job posting to a job board (Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc.).
5. Recruiter reviews candidate resumes for the qualifications the HM wants.
6. Those resumes pass a pre-screen (optional) and get sent to the HM.
7. The HM interviews candidates.
8. An offer is made to the best candidate. If no candidate meets the HM's criteria, the process restarts at step 5 (an HM would rather make no hire than a "bad hire").

## 3. The Philosophy of a Resume
* **The resume's only job is to get you interviews**.
* Good resumes that get interviews read like government forms turned in at the DMV.
* **Resume Quality Metric (Application-to-Interview Ratio)**:
  * `1 to 10` = A+
  * `1 to 25` = B
  * `1 to 50` = C-
  * `1 to 75` = D
  * `1 to 100+` = F

### What a Resume Is vs. Isn't
* **Resumes are**:
  * Made to showcase that you meet the minimum qualifications for the role.
  * Tailored to a specific job title.
* **Resumes are terrible at**:
  * Showing brags.
  * Marketing.
  * Graphic design.

## 4. The Most Important Step: Finding Keywords
Before writing the first words on your resume, you must find the **keywords** for your job title.
* **Keywords** are shorthand for qualifications that both the Recruiter and HM look for.
* Both the Recruiter and HM take roughly **15 seconds** to review your resume. They need to see **HOW** and **WHY** you used those keywords in those 15 seconds, or you will be rejected.

### Job Titles and Job Descriptions (JD)
* **Job Title**: Common name for a certain set of qualifications (e.g., Barista = POS, Customer Service, Basic Math; IT Helpdesk = Customer Service, Troubleshooting, MS Office, Zoom, Windows).
* **Job Description (JD)**: Contains title, salary, day-to-day duties, and EEO statements. **Ignore all of that—the only thing in the JD that matters is the Qualifications**.

### How to Identify and Collect Qualifications
Qualifications are found in bullet points under sections titled:
* Requirements
* Qualifications
* Must Have / Need to Have
* What we are Looking for / We Would Love To Meet You If / You Might Be a Great Fit If

**Step-by-step keyword collection**:
1. Search Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc.
2. Find a "Good JD" where you meet 75% of the qualifications, and record the keywords.
3. Repeat 10 to 15 times for JDs with the exact same job title.
4. Tally how many times each qualification appears.
5. The most frequent qualifications form your target list. (Resumes are title-specific; different titles require different resumes).

## 5. Formatting Rules

* **Font**: Arial only (do not use any other font).
* **Colors**: Black and white only (phone, email, and LinkedIn/portfolio links may be blue).
* **Columns**: Single column only.
* **Line Spacing**: 1.5.
* **Length**: It is fine if the resume is longer than one page.
* **Photos**: No pictures of yourself.

---

## 6. Resume Structure & Sections

### Section 1: Contact Information (Center Aligned)
* First and Last Name: Font size 14, **Bold**.
* Phone Number, Email, and LinkedIn/Portfolio: Font size 12.
* Location: Include "US Citizen" or "Green Card Holder" (if applicable) alongside City and State, Font size 12.
* Languages: Place bilingual/multilingual skills beside or below the last line.

### Optional: Summary Section
* **Only use if**:
  1. Changing industries.
  2. Relocating/moving out of state.
  3. On a VISA requiring sponsorship explanation (not needed for OPT or H1B without an approved I-140).
* If none of these apply, **do not write a summary**. Do not label it "Summary"; place it directly under contact info.

### Section 2: Education & Certificates (Optional / Top or Bottom)
* **Alignment**: Left aligned, 10.5 font.
* **Header**: **Education & Certificates** (bolded; nothing else in this section bolded).
* **Length**: 3 lines maximum, bullet points only.
* **Details**: Degree, subject, and institution.
  * Graduated over 3 years ago: Write `Status - Graduated`.
  * Graduated under 3 years ago: Include graduation year.
* Active relevant certificates can be listed without dates.
* Move this section to the bottom if the target job does not require a degree or if the degree makes you overqualified.

### Section 3: Work History, Internships, and Projects
* **Definitions**:
  * Paid by a company = **Work History** or **Internships**.
  * Unpaid = **Projects** (Projects do not have dates; maximum 3 bullet points per project).
* **Order of Importance**: Relevance to target job > Work History > Internships > Projects.
* **Formatting**:
  * Font size 10.5, left aligned, reverse chronological order.
  * Header (**Work History** / **Projects**) bolded.
  * Job title, company, and state are *italicized* (left-aligned).
  * Dates of employment are *italicized* (right-aligned), including Month and Year (e.g., `June 2023 to Current`).

## 7. Writing Bullet Points (What, How, Result/Reason)

Every job entry requires 3 to 8 bullet points total:
1. **Bullet 1 (Job Summary)**: One past-tense summary sentence describing the role, written so someone without industry knowledge understands it. Uses 1 period max and 3 lines max.
2. **Bullets 2 to 8 (Experience Bullets)**: Written in the **What, How, Result/Reason** formula:
   * **What**: The keywords/skills used.
   * **How**: The specific action taken.
   * **Result/Reason**: The measurable outcome or reason for the action.
   * *Rules*: Past tense always, 3 lines maximum, no more than 1 period per bullet.

*Goal*: Place **75% of your target keywords in the top half of the first page** of your resume. Limit total work history to 12 years maximum.

## 8. Where and How to Apply (Beating the ATS)

### Top Job Boards
* **LinkedIn.com & Indeed.com**: The two primary job boards containing the vast majority of legitimate listings.
* **Craigslist.org**: 95% scams, but the 5% real jobs are hyper-local and never posted anywhere else.
* *Other aggregators*: Mostly repost from Indeed and LinkedIn.

### How the ATS Works & Why Speed Matters
* **Rule #1**: **The faster you apply, the better your chances of getting an interview**.
* Most ATS platforms (Workday, ADP, Taleo) review applicants on a **first-come, first-served** queue.
* Recruiters manage 35–70 open positions at once. Once they find enough qualified resumes (e.g., reaching candidate #75 out of 150), they schedule interviews and stop reading later submissions.
* AI resume scanning is rare in practice (~1 in 100 applications outside of dedicated AI firms).

### Tactical Search Settings
* **Remote Search**: Set location to `United States` rather than your specific city to avoid artificially filtering roles out.
* **Indeed**: Always switch search sort from "relevance" to **"Date"**.
* **LinkedIn**: Filter by **"Most recent"**. If using AI search, explicitly query:  
  `[JOB TITLE] jobs with under 100 applicants`.

## 9. F.A.Q. Quick Reference
* **Why is hiring difficult right now?** Driven by the economy and recession conditions where applicants outnumber open positions.
* **Does this format work across fields?** Works for almost every profession (except Medical Doctors and Lawyers).
* **Do ghost jobs exist?** "Ghost jobs" are mostly narratives driven by resume sites selling products; actual scams exist, but legitimate companies post to hire.
* **International use**: Valid in the US, Canada, Mexico, India, EU, UK, and Africa (the author notes the Netherlands as an exception).
